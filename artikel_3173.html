Ichor's Profit Jumps as Cash Burn Grows: Rapid Earnings Reaction

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

A look at what's driving the chip-equipment supplier's margin gains, why its cash pile shrank, and what the fourth-quarter outlook really says about earnings.

Ichor Holdings (ICHR -5.30%) earned $20.9 million in its fiscal third quarter, a sharp turn from a $22.9 million loss a year ago. Shares rose about 3% in early after-hours trading. The sales ramp now produces real profit, but it's eating cash as inventory and unpaid customer bills pile up.

Ichor builds the gas delivery systems inside chipmaking tools, and its plants carry heavy fixed costs. When volume climbs, those costs spread across more units and margins rise. Management also credits shifting work into its main factories and selling more of its own parts. Operating costs fell even as sales grew, so operating margin nearly tripled from last quarter, to 6.9%.

The catch is cash. Inventory and receivables together soaked up about $75 million in the quarter. That's partly normal for a supplier gearing up. But receivables grew much faster than sales, which suggests slower customer payments or shipments bunched late in the quarter. Last quarter's stock sale gives Ichor room.

The fourth-quarter outlook calls for more sales growth, but the GAAP earnings midpoint is $0.55 -- flat with this quarter. Management's promise of rising earnings rests on adjusted figures. This quarter's GAAP profit got help from a light tax bill and lower stock-based compensation, and the outlook assumes that expense rises again.

Watch whether free cash flow turns positive as the ramp matures. Also check whether receivables grow in line with sales.
