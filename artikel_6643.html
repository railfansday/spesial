One More Bite

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Here at Taste of Home, there's nothing we love more than trying a new recipe. But sometimes that means buying an ingredient or two that isn't part of our regular dinner rotation—which can mean leftover condiments, sauces or spices we don't know what to do with. The question is: What's the next bite?

That's where One More Bite comes in. We're exploring how to use one ingredient in multiple ways, so nothing goes to waste. A single new ingredient can transform weeknight meals, weekend favorites and simple snacks, and might just find a permanent spot on our grocery list.

We often see chili crisp spooned over dumplings, soups and rice bowls, but there are so many uses for this crunchy, fiery oil-based sauce. We love mixing chili crisp into biscuit batter, layering it on grilled cheese sandwiches and cooking it with eggs.

Many people buy tahini for dips like hummus or baba ghanoush, then wonder what to do with the rest of the jar. Not us. The creamy sesame paste never lasts long in our kitchen—it blends beautifully into sweet and savory recipes.

Hot honey didn't exactly invent sweet heat—but it sure did make it easy to drizzle on everything. This swicy condiment is sweet enough to hook you and spicy enough to keep you coming back.

Pistachio cream is like a green version of Nutella, but sweeter, more spreadable and decidedly more decadent. When pistachio cream went viral alongside Dubai chocolate, it sparked a wave of creative recipes that lean into its rich, nutty flavor.

Served raw, the punchy flavor of kimchi cuts through rich foods, lightening up dishes like mac and cheese or burgers. Cooked, its acidity mellows and the umami notes deepen, turning a last-minute stir-fry or grain bowl into a dish that tastes layered and intentional.

When we think of pesto as a garlicky herb sauce, the ways to use it become endless. We've spooned it over garlic bread, tucked it into a stuffed chicken breast and whisked it into mayo for a quick sandwich spread.

Brittle sheets of rice paper become bendable and stretchy when dipped in water, allowing them to be wrapped around all kinds of fillings. They have a chewy finish when served fresh, a crunchy texture when fried, and a wafer-like bite when dipped in egg and stacked.

Nothing swirls, spreads or steals the spotlight quite like our favorite retro ingredient: marshmallow creme. No matter the recipe, marshmallow creme brings its signature lightness and gooey pull, adding a soft finish that never goes out of style.

Za'atar, a Middle Eastern spice blend, tastes woodsy, tart, bright and crunchy all at once. There are plenty of traditional recipes that call for za'atar, some as simple as mixing it with oil to make a dip. But it can also be used more broadly, in place of a single dried herb.

When a stew we had been simmering for hours tasted flat, we added a spoonful of miso and the broth took on a richness it had previously lacked. Once we started using it more, we realized that it belongs in sweet and salty desserts, easy sides and main dishes that need a little oomph.

Because it needs to be harvested by hand and yields only a tiny amount, saffron is one of the most expensive ingredients you can buy—so the last thing you want to do is let it sit in your pantry and collect dust.

You don't need to save fish sauce for your favorite Thai recipes or other Southeast Asian dishes. We sprinkle it into marinara sauce and Caesar dressing, when more salt would just make it salty instead of adding depth.

Reducing balsamic vinegar concentrates the sugars, creating a sweet, tangy reduction. Balsamic glaze brightens up watermelon salads, adds nuance to grilled cheese and creates a dazzling finish for baked chicken.
