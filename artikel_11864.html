Ref Watch: Three John Brooks blunders in Newcastle vs Everton

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Senior Correspondent AUTHORITY Senior football journalist specialising in refereeing and officiating; former contributor to The Football League Paper, Late Tackle, and the Premier League; University of Derby graduate. FOCUS Refereeing controversies, football finance and governance, PGMOL decisions, and officiating analysis across the Premier League, EFL, and SPFL. THE INSIGHT Harri utilises a network of officiating and industry contacts to deliver verified, expert analysis of refereeing decisions and PGMOL policy. He provides the regulatory clarity behind the controversy to ensure fans get the full picture.

John Brooks was the man in the middle as Newcastle United and Everton clashed in a tight affair at St James' Park.

Emotions were expected to bubble over on Monday, 2 November, after the two sides went head-to-head in the Carabao Cup during the week.

James Tarkowski and Joelinton were amongst those involved in a flashpoint in the previous meeting, and those sparks threatened to explode again.

For the most part, the referee had things under control, at least until he was forced to send off Jacob Ramsey deep into stoppage time.

Brennan Johnson and Fabian Schar joined him in the referee's notebook, and more probably should have followed due to the mass confrontation.

Thierno Barry's goal made all the difference in the North East, but some fans were left talking about the referee's performance more than anything else.

Very few have argued against Ramsey's red card. Anyone who has is clearly looking for a reaction or genuinely doesn't know the rules of football.

It was an incredibly dangerous challenge on Harrison Armstrong, but Lewis Hall clearly didn't see it that way in the aftermath of the red card.

Eager to get an equaliser in stoppage time, he angered multiple Everton players and fans by attempting to pick Armstrong up. Such a stupid move, and one that should have seen him cautioned.

Ramsey was sent off for two bookable offences in quick succession, first having his name taken for a cynical foul on Hayden Hackney.

Just moments later, he was given his marching orders, but even Keith Hackett, a former PGMOL chief, was stunned at the decision.

And not because he didn't think it was a foul. No, the former referee wanted to see a straight red card for that kind of challenge.

Right before Barry's opener, he was caught up in an incident with Joelinton, who, let's be honest, is known for getting too rough on the pitch.

However, on this occasion, the referee clearly fell for the Everton striker's play-acting, throwing himself to the floor rather theatrically and buying a free-kick.

A caution for Joelinton would have been even worse, but the Brazilian escaped, and good riddance. Brooks had already been deceived for the free-kick.
