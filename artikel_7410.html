Media Briefing: Publishers are selling more subscriptions to smaller audiences. The midterms are the next test.

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Election season is a familiar subscription play for news publishers. With the U.S. midterms a week away, they’re looking once again at how to turn renewed political attention into paying readers.

“Growth depends on attracting and converting casual readers, and that is getting harder,” said Greg Piechota, researcher-in-residence at INMA. “Social media and search send fewer people to news sites every year. A big news event is one of the few remaining moments when casual readers come to a news brand on their own, so what happens in those weeks matters more than it used to.”

Declining referral traffic may make it harder for publishers to reach casual readers, but the picture isn’t all bad. Even when site traffic shrinks for publishers, subscriptions can still grow, Piechota noted.

The typical publisher worldwide now reaches a fifth fewer people a month than in 2019. And in North America, a fifth fewer than at the 2024 election peak. However, the typical publisher sells 50% to 85% more subscriptions a quarter than in 2019, to that smaller audience, he said.

Case in point: On Monday, new subscriptions rose 50% at Reuters, primarily in the U.S. and around coverage of the Brazil election and from Reuters’ live blog, said Josh London, head of Reuters Professional, who oversees the direct-to-consumer and direct-to-professional businesses. Reuters launched its paywall two years ago.

“We expect a similar pattern around the Israel election this week. That demand for fast, reliable coverage for the U.S. midterms, we would expect to be a driver as well,” London said.

While Reuters is not making any changes to its dynamic paywall, London said it is bolstering its coverage with a U.S. newsroom expansion (including new hires to its White House, national security and investigative teams), a content bundle partnership with the USA Today Network, polls from an Ipsos partnership, and a Washington D.C. “war room” covering key races.

“Always, but especially with elections, readers turn to Reuters for trusted, unbiased election news at speed, and that trust and that independent, non-national coverage translates into new subscriptions,” London said.

The last 2024 U.S. presidential election did move sales in North America: the typical North American publisher sold 43% more subscriptions than in 2019, and 17% more than in the 2020 election quarter, according to INMA’s analysis of 3,018 news brands, 79 of which are in North America.

Regardless, as AI reshapes how readers encounter information, publishers are betting that trusted election coverage can give them a reason to come directly to news brands, and keep coming back.

A tried-and-tested tactic established subscription businesses have long relied on adjusting paywalls around major news events to open up coverage and reach more readers, or tightening access to drive subscriptions.

The New York Times is taking the open-access route once again: it has made its midterm election coverage free in The Times app, with no paywall until the results are clear, a Times spokesperson told Digiday. Readers who download the app get access to the Times’ reporting, explainers and poll trackers. While not specifically a subscription conversion initiative, the idea is to draw in readers interested in following midterm election coverage and retain them.

“The midterms give us an opportunity to demonstrate why Politico’s journalism matters to the people who rely on it for their work, both in the lead-up to election night and long afterwards,” said Cally Baute, chief commercial officer at Politico. “We want people who discover us through election coverage to understand the value of that specialist intelligence in the decisions they make every day.”

On Nov. 3, five of Politico’s top political journalists will provide live election analysis on the publisher’s website, app and YouTube.

“We’ll carry that approach into other consequential elections, including France’s presidential contest in 2027. Our investments in journalism and technology are designed to make our original reporting more useful to readers and demonstrate why a subscription remains valuable long after the results are in,” Baute added.

CNN, which launched its digital subscription All Access last year, has expanded its offering to the U.K. and Canada ahead of the midterm elections. The news org sees elections as a “major subscription driver,” according to a person familiar with CNN’s strategy. While they declined to share details, they noted that CNN has executed a full marketing campaign across most of its consumer touchpoints, and has been working to drive up subscriptions since earlier this year around the primary elections.

Naturally, the real test will come after election night: whether readers who came for the midterms have a reason to stay.

“The election is the hook, not the habit. The work after a political spike should be focused on breadth: onboarding that steers people to other news, sport, lifestyle and newsletters, and the bundled non-news products, such as games,” Piechota said.

Meta says it will consult publishers before finalizing its new terms

Following mounting backlash over its proposed terms allowing broad scraping of publishers’ websites, Meta has pledged to consult with news industry leaders before implementing the changes, potentially delaying their Nov. 20 rollout, Nieman Lab reported.

Norwegian publishers sue Meta over plans to use their journalism for AI

A coalition of 10 Norwegian media organizations has asked the Oslo District Court to block Meta’s new commercial terms before they take effect, Euronews reported.

Vox launches app to grow paid memberships

Vox has launched its first mobile app, bringing articles, videos and podcasts into one destination, Nieman Lab reported. The app is free to download and videos and podcasts are accessible, but reading articles and listening to podcasts ad-free requires a paid membership.

The Verge plans to launch an app next year to build a more direct relationship with its audience amid a considerable decline in website traffic, A Media Operator reported.

Axel Springer CEO wanted to buy TikTok’s European arm

Mathias Döpfner, CEO and co-owner of Germany’s Axel Springer, pitched a deal to buy the European arm of TikTok, The Financial Times reported.
