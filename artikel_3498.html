'IT: Welcome to Derry' Quietly Drops an Essential Stephen King Lore Episode

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Editor's note: The below contains spoilers for "A Story of Derry."

The irony that fans of Stranger Things, a show heavily inspired by IT, spent months convincing themselves there was a secret episode is somewhat amusing when considering that the prequel series IT: Welcome to Derry managed to drop a surprise episode on Halloween at midnight. Although the first season of It: Welcome to Derry took place in the 1960s, two decades before the events of Stephen King's original novel, Season 2 is expected to go back further in the timeline to the 1930s during the Great Depression. The show's new prequel episode, a standalone installment titled “A Story of Derry,” goes back even further than that into pre-Colonial times, where the Western Wood hasn't yet been founded as Derry.

The most powerful idea in IT: Welcome to Derry is that the ancient evil doesn't take just one form, as it represents a societal danger that reconfigures itself within different generations based on the context of the era. “A Story of Derry” explains how the spirit known as “The Galloo” was first confined to the land that would eventually become Derry, even if its evil had already caused mass destruction. A core tenet of King’s stories is the idea that there will always be a group of brave, often young outsiders who are willing to take a stand against these supernatural forces, and “A Story of Derry” features a compelling ensemble of new protagonists in Necani (Kiawentiio), Maneese (Michael Podemski-Bedard), and Kino (Isabel Deroy-Olson), the warriors from the Shokopiwah tribe who go hunting for the Galloo.

If the Galloo and the Shokopiwah tribe sound familiar, it’s because they were first introduced in the Season 1 episode “The Great Swirling Apparatus of Our Planet's Function,” where Dick Halloran (Chris Chalk) is working under the orders of Lieutenant General Shaw (James Remar) to interrogate the Native American teenager Taniel (Joshua Odjick) to learn about his aunt’s story about how the Galloo first came to Earth and was eventually imprisoned by their people using a fragment from the star. “A Story of Derry” offers more detail to this origin tale by telling it from the right perspective; instead of this critical piece of Native American mythology being coaxed out of a victim through militaristic interrogation, it is shown from the perspective of the young warriors of the Shokopiwah tribe who would go on to pass it along through oral traditions. Colonialism is a component of how The Galloo reconfigures itself, which is hinted at in “A Story of Derry;” not only does it first take the form of white colonizers who have impeded on the land, but it is implied that The Galloo was awakened by the military in an act of appropriating Native American magic for their own gain.

EPs Andy Muschietti and Barbara Muschietti also talk about that gruesome car ride, the movie theater massacre, and holding back on Pennywise.

One of the bigger surprises in “A Story of Derry” is that the episode includes a great performance from Zahn McClarnon as Mativ, a Native American warrior who initially explains the story of the Galloo to Necani and their friends. The revelation that Mativ has been corrupted by the Galloo, which led him to massacre his own tribe, is a reminder of how cursed the land is to experience acts of violence that leave few survivors, allowing the stories to be lost. It’s also a showcase for McClarnon's terrifically unsettling acting, because Mativ is initially presented as being a caring mentor before revealing himself to be compromised. McClarnon does a great job of showing Mativ's complexity as the character struggles to resist the corrupting influence within him.

The most shocking reveal in “A Story of Derry” is that it marks the first on-screen appearance of Maturin, the cosmic turtle with the power to defeat the Galloo. Although the idea of an all-powerful turtle might seem ridiculous to those who aren’t versed in King’s lore, this character is of critical importance within many of the author’s most legendary texts; Maturin is one of the 12 ancient guardians in The Dark Tower universe responsible for holding up the pillars of reality, and in the original IT novel, Bill recites a ritual taught to him by Maturin to expel Pennywise. It’s been suspected that IT: Welcome to Derry might delve into some of the weirder aspects of King’s mythology and potentially incorporate aspects of some of his other novels, and the introduction of Maturin is the first clue.
