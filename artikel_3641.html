Brittany Snow Recalls When She Developed Eating Disorder, Details Recovery

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Brittany Snow is looking back at her long road to recovery after struggling with an eating disorder.

The Hunting Wives star began to battle anorexia, exercise bulimia, depression and self-harm after landing her breakout role in the soap opera Guiding Light at 12 years old. She entered an inpatient rehab facility at age 19 but left early to film John Tucker Must Die. At 23, Snow took a life-saving break from Hollywood and completed a year of intensive outpatient therapy.

Snow spoke extensively about her eating disorder battle and mental health on Dax Shepard’s “Armchair Expert” podcast, weeks after she celebrated 17 years of recovery.

“Seventeen years ago, I didn’t know if I would make it. I couldn’t see a future, and I certainly couldn’t imagine one where I would learn to be kind to myself,” she shared via Instagram in September 2026. “If someone had told me then that I would not only live, but one day have a beautiful, full life, free from the cycle of self-destruction and my eating disorder… I truly don’t think I would have believed them.”

Snow’s struggles started when she was starring as Susan “Daisy” Lema on Guiding Light at age 12.

“This gets a little dark … I decided to start emulating the women on set, and they were all on diets, and they all were all looking at their bodies and talking about their bodies,” she recalled. “My body was changing, and I thought, ‘I don’t want to be a chunky teenager,’ so I start dieting, and then it starts becoming this sort of best friend, and definitely like an addiction where I was really hyper-focused on that being the only thing I cared about.”

She continued, “So it was just work and … counting every single calorie, working out for two hours after rehearsals before I would shoot the day of soap opera acting, and so it was repetitive, and it made me feel safe that I got a high of, like, every time I got on the scale, there was a new number, and I did well if it went down.”

Snow quietly battled her eating disorder while starring as Meg Pryor on the NBC series American Dreams from 2002 to 2005.

“I knew that the show was so good, and I really relied on that. But I was so sick at that point with my eating disorder that I was really trying to just get by,” she shared.

Snow revealed that it was her father, John, who convinced her to go to therapy.

“My dad actually [intervened], and that was the first time that I went to therapy. I started therapy when I was 16. I started going to program, and I learned what an eating disorder was,” she recalled.

Snow added that a magazine article opened her eyes to the severity of what she was going through.

“I read an article in a magazine about this woman who had an eating disorder, and I was like, ‘The way she’s counting her food and the way she’s talking about her body, that’s exactly what I do.’ And I said, ‘Eating disorder? I don’t even know what that means,’” she continued. “And so I ripped out the magazine. I carried it around in my back pocket for a while because it sort of signified that somebody had an answer to this, and it made me feel not alone.”

Snow said she found the most success in her recovery when she enrolled in an outpatient program.

“The thing that worked for me the most was an outpatient situation where I quit acting for a year, and I was in an outpatient program with this guy named Ray Santa Maria, who I love, at One to One Treatment. Shout-out Ray. He saved my life,” she recalled.

The Pitch Perfect star continued, “And it was dealing with doing normal human things: going to a restaurant, going out to eat with your friends, going to the movie theater, and not knowing if you should get popcorn or not. You know, things like that where you had to be really accountable and not lie, which is, you know, we love to lie, and we love to be sneaky about things, and to just really show up for yourself and trust yourself. And that’s what I learned with outpatient. … For a year, I was just, like, with the thing that I did every day, which was like immersed in [it], and I think that’s what really helped me is because I learned to trust myself every day.”

Snow realized she needed to get better while directing her 2023 film Parachute.

“I directed a movie a couple years ago called Parachute, and that was really about the beginning of figuring out how to get better and the final day that I got better,” she said.
