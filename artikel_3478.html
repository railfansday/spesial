NBC Cements St. Denis Medical’s Place In Network Comedy Hierarchy | Review

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

NBC's St. Denis Medical return for season 3 with some changes for the Oregon hospital's emergency department, but the comedy remains consistent.

Laura Hurley has been writing about the entertainment industry for more than twelve years, after a decade as a content producer and editor at CinemaBlend after writing for WhatCulture and Examiner. Attending events like SCAD TVfest and San Diego Comic-Con over the course of her career, she has been immersed in the world of network, streaming, and cable TV.

The motley crew of St. Denis Medical 's doctors and nurses are officially at work in primetime yet again thanks to the season 3 premiere. The sitcom brought the mockumentary format back to NBC, filling the void left by shows like Parks & Recreation and The Office. That's not to say that St. Denis only became a hit because of the format, and the network cancelled Stumble despite its mockumentary status at the end of the 2025-2026 TV season. The pressure was on for season 3 to prove whether the Allison Tolman-starring sitcom could recapture the magic for a third season.

The second season of St. Denis Medical technically didn't end on a cliffhanger, so there was no twist to keep fans on the edges of their seats to see resolved when the comedy returned. NBC's fall 2026 schedule also saved St. Denis (as well as the new season of Tracy Morgan's The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins) for nearly a full month after most of its biggest fall premieres, and more than a week after NBC's other two comedies premiered. ABC's Scrubs reboot got in much earlier on the hospital-set comedy this fall.

Fortunately, St. Denis Medical picked up on what worked from the end of season 2. The show has been channeling the quality of comedy of The Office from the beginning, without actually following Steve Carell's hit to the letter. The season 3 premiere, called "He Ain't Noah Wyle," proves that the mockumentary format is still a perfect fit for an underfunded hospital, particularly with a cast of comedy heavy-hitters like Tolman, Wendi McLendon-Covey, David Alan Grier, and the rest of the ensemble.

Despite a lack of cliffhanger from the end of season 2, there were two plotlines that needed to be picked up in season 3. The most pressing was the fact that Ron (David Alan Grier) underwent an intense bypass surgery, which was serious enough to cut down on the laughs from one of primetime's funniest shows back in the spring. On the brighter side of the season 2 finale, the tension between Serena (Kahyun Kim) and Matt (Mekki Leeper) finally led to them starting a relationship after sharing a kiss.

To the comedy's credit, the premiere wasted no time in paying off what the finale set up instead of dragging the twists out indefinitely. That was sometimes an issue for The Office in the earliest days of NBC's mockumentary dominance, particularly with Jim and Pam's romance from season 2 to season 3. St. Denis Medical is already handling Matt and Serena's romance with more of a deft hand without abandoning it completely or purely playing it for laughs.

ScreenRant interviews stars Allison Tolman, Wendi Mclendon-Covey, and David Alan Grier about the St. Denis Medical finale's major cliffhanger.

The question of Dr. Ron's future at the hospital was far more pressing. Despite the talent of the full cast at bringing the comedy to the Oregon hospital, Grier's comedic star power drove a lot of the laughs over the first two seasons. While it was unlikely that St. Denis Medical would use his character's health scare to write him out, there were some stakes about his future. The good news is that the show didn't drag out the question, and not just because Grier was heavily featured in the trailer for season 3.

"He Ain't Noah Wyle" paid off on the set-up from season 2 that Ron was facing a serious recovery from his surgery, to the extent that he was still in physical therapy in the season 3 premiere. In true Ron fashion, he was milking his PT for all it was worth, but St. Denis didn't underplay what he went through. The show also cast the perfect person as his temporary replacement: Damon Wayans, guest-starring as Dr. Highland.

Even though Ron and Dr. Highland didn't share too many moments, St. Denis Medical did honor their history as In Living Color cast members with a memorable scene before the final credits rolled and Wayans exited as a doctor.

While the season 3 premiere nailed the payoff of Matt and Serena getting together, "He Ain't Noah Wyle" fell short when it came to Joyce and Ron. The two characters have the longest history of any other duo on St. Denis Medical, and the sitcom has made the most of their relationship in certain arcs across the first two seasons. David Alan Grier and Wendi McLendon-Covey were also the cast's biggest names in comedy from the very beginning, and the comedy seemed to be igniting some sparks between them in the final moments of season 2.

St. Denis Medical's Ron seems to have feelings for the hospital's executive director, Joyce. Will they eventually become more than friends?

Ron calling for "My Joyce" while coming out of his post-surgery drugged stupor wasn't played for laughs at the end of season 2, with Alex (Allison Tolman) and Bruce (Josh Lawson) both reacting with interest rather than cracking up. It was played as a meaningful moment, and the reveal occurring at the very end of the finale suggested that St. Denis Medical was setting up another romance for season 3.

The premiere did address what happened, but Ron made it clear that he's not interested in taking the story any further. After the off-screen documentary crew showed him the footage, he demanded that the video not be shown to anybody else, and looked about as serious as Ron has ever looked over three seasons. St. Denis Medical clearly can handle relationships well, as proved by Matt and Serena. The premiere suggests that the groundwork that was set for Joyce and Ron isn't going to be explored, despite the promise at the end of season 2.

The title of the premiere indicated that the most successful current medical drama on TV was going to be referenced by what is arguably TV's funniest medical show. Alex didn't disappoint in establishing why "He Ain't Noah Wyle" was a worthy title for the episode. Clearly still unhappy about Dr. Highland replacing Ron, Alex pointed out that Ron's replacement isn't quite on par with Noah Wyle's on The Pitt. The episode made it clear that Dr. Highland's similarities to Dr. Robby on the HBO's drama weren't all in Alex's head. either; Highland directly quoted Wyle's character.

She also made it quite clear that she has a crush on Noah Wyle, and the timing couldn't have been better for that reveal. Wyle was named as People's Sexiest Man Alive just days before St. Denis Medical returned for season 3. While the writers couldn't have known when they were planning the season 3 premiere that Noah Wyle would get that annual honor, Alex's crush was another win for St. Denis' history of pop culture references. Season 3 is off to a strong start in that department, and history suggests that the comedy will keep the trend going.

New episodes of St. Denis Medical will air on Mondays at 8 p.m. ET on NBC, and begin streaming the next day on Peacock.
