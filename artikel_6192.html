Flying Around Russia Is Testing Airlines’ Climate Targets

Avoiding Russia has become a climate problem for airlines heading from Europe to Northeast Asia — and a competitive disadvantage. Finnair, one of the most affected airlines, says it’s committed to its climate goals.

Finnair flights to Northeast Asia now take about a third longer than they did before Russia closed its airspace in 2022, using up more fuel and spewing more emissions.

Russia barred airlines from EU countries and the U.K. from its airspace in retaliation for their bans on Russian carriers after its invasion of Ukraine. Airlines from Japan, South Korea, and Taiwan have largely avoided Russian airspace over safety concerns. This cut off many of the most efficient routes between Europe and Northeast Asia.

On routes between Europe and Northeast Asia, carriers that are barred from or avoid Russian airspace burn 13.8% more fuel per flight than rivals that still fly over it, according to an analysis by Skailark. The firm compared fuel burn on identical routes and aircraft types in 2019 and from March 2025 to June 2026.

Nearly four years after Russia closed its airspace to EU and UK carriers, the cost is now quantified: airlines barred from or avoiding Russian airspace burn 13.8% more fuel per flight on Europe–Northeast Asia routes than rivals that still overfly Russia, according to an analysis by Skailark comparing identical routes and aircraft types in 2019 and from March 2025 to June 2026. The extra burn adds 1.53 million metric tons of CO2 a year, or 12% of those flights' total emissions. Finnair is the most exposed carrier, with Northeast Asia flights taking 33.2% longer and burning 33.5% more fuel; sustainability VP Riku Aho said company-level gross CO2 emissions are up roughly 10% since 2019, with knock-on effects on fleet, navigation, crew, and aircraft utilization costs. ANA, Japan Airlines, Air France, KLM, and Lufthansa are also among the most affected. Meanwhile carriers from China, the Gulf, and Türkiye that still use Russian airspace are adding European connections — Emirates recently launched Helsinki — while British Airways suspended London–Beijing and Virgin Atlantic dropped Shanghai in 2024. A Lufthansa spokesperson called the situation a structural competitive disadvantage for European carriers. With jet fuel at $4.59 per gallon and the EU's SAF blending mandate underway, Finnair is not planning for Russian airspace to reopen soon.
