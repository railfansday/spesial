HPE’s CEO says his company has ‘zero exposure’ to AI’s financing web

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Good morning. It’s Election Day here in the U.S., and a shout-out to all of our readers who will make time to vote today, if they haven’t already done so. Several of my editorial colleagues and I shared our thoughts on what’s at stake for business leaders in this segment of the Fortune Daily show. (It starts at the 17:30 mark.) From AI policy to affordability measures, the political outcomes will shape how the U.S. and its companies compete.

The question is how much. Business leaders have long had to navigate a myriad of policies at the state and federal levels. Right now, concerns over valuations and a possible capital crunch are arguably worrying investors more than who controls Congress. While the frontier labs face growing scrutiny over their models and opaque financing, many CEOs are prioritizing security, safety, and speed because it’s good for business.I’m heading back from the annual gathering of senior leaders at Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) in Miami this morning, where I spoke about leadership trends and also interviewed HPE CEO Antonio Neri about his take on the current business landscape. It’s been a good year for the enterprise IT solutions business that spun off from HP in 2015, with HP Inc. retaining the printer and PC business. HPE recently cleared the last legal hurdle to complete its $14 billion acquisition of Juniper Networks. While its stock price has lost a quarter of its value over the past month, it’s still up 112% so far this year.Like a lot of business leaders I meet, Neri is quick to distance himself from the fear of a bubble around AI. “We have zero exposure to that financing architecture that people are concerned about,” he told me, referring to the circular equity, funding, and supply deals between chipmakers, hyperscalers, AI labs, and cloud providers. “We took a more brutal approach at the beginning of the cycle through policies on where not to go … 2026 has been the year where we have built a foundation. We have a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity to break away. This is the window.”

Anthropic’s growth slows as higher costs push it back to an operating loss

Anthropic’s revenue reached $17 billion in the third quarter, up 47% from the previous quarter. That’s a slowdown from the 144% growth it posted in the second quarter. The company’s updated IPO filing, which included the third quarter numbers, showed a $567 million operating loss as computing costs rose, though the company remained profitable on an adjusted basis.

Apple beats sales expectations despite rising memory chip costs

Apple reported $114.5 billion in quarterly revenue and $30.7 billion in profit, beating Wall Street expectations in John Ternus’s first earnings report as CEO. Rising memory chip costs remain a concern.

Palantir’s quarterly revenue rose 96% to $2.3 billion, beating Wall Street expectations as its U.S. commercial business overtook U.S. government revenue for the first time. Shares fell 3.6% in after-hours trading, despite the company increasing its full-year revenue forecast.
