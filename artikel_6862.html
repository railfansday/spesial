A great sound system doesn’t have to be complicated. This is all you need.

Bass is such an integral part of the movie going experience. It’s the part of sound you can feel. You go to the movies to feel the waves crashing against Odysseus’s ship. This used to be nigh on impossible to replicate at home, unless you were paying a serious sound system company to come trick out the movie room. Now? All you need is a soundbar that plays nice with a paired-up subwoofer.

What I’ve learned in my years testing audio equipment, is that even the best soundbars are only so good without a subwoofer. If you really want to emulate a theater or concert at home and get that Dolby Atmos surround experience, you need the power of bass. A subwoofer is there to deliver that deep, booming hit that turns your movie, music, or gaming into something you don't just hear but feel too.

The best soundbar-and-subwoofer combos are typically sold separately, which is often frustrating because mixing and matching isn’t really a thing—especially if you want a wireless subwoofer. That’s why today, I’ve singled out the best package combos that come with both components. What to Consider When Buying a Soundbar Combo

I could dive deep into the tech specs of soundbars with subwoofers, but the small differences between brands have a minuscule impact on sound quality. Instead, look for these important features.

Subwoofer Size: Subwoofers are an important part of the sonic experience because they deliver the bass. That low rumble or thumping beat you feel in your bones is the result of a powerful sub. Subwoofer speakers range from six to 16 inches. For most midsize rooms, eight inches is optimal.

Wireless Streaming: We get it. Speakers aren't just for watching Top Gun: Maverick for the 17th time. Music matters too, and the ability to wirelessly stream your music from your phone or computer directly to your soundbar and subwoofer matters a whole lot to some people. I won't lie: I love it too. It makes putting on a party playlist a breeze.

Smart Capabilities: Building off that last one, people want soundbars that will integrate with their Google smart home. Most mid- to high-end options these days will have AirPlay, Alexa, and Chromecast built in. All of the ones I selected here do.

What are audio channels, and how many do I need?

The sound-channel numbers indicate how many different places the sound is coming from. It's like the 21st-century version of mono versus stereo. Each bar and sub has multiple speakers, each sometimes serving up audio on multiple channels. When you see a number like 5.1.4 or 11.1.4 (sometimes with the last .4 omitted), it's referring to the number of channels. The first digit is the important one, with 5 and 7 being the most common. The more channels your audio setup has, the more sound it can produce.

Now you know why we say, for example, that Samsung's 11-channel soundbar is going above and beyond. For most people, a 5.1.4-capable surround system with a subwoofer will be plenty, regardless of if they're are a sports fan, gamer, hip-hop head, or cinephile.

Dolby Atmos (and DTS:X, Sony 360 Reality, and Eclipsa—among others) is basically just a branded buzzword for a new immersive 3D audio technology. Surround sound without the need for extra speakers. It doesn't use traditional audio channels. Any speaker can attempt to play this format, but the soundbars and subwoofers we picked are designed with Dolby Atmos or DTS:X in mind.
