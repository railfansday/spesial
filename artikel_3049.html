Amazon's Report Passed Both Tests I Set for It. I'd Buy More of the Stock Now.

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The e-commerce and cloud giant's report cleared both hurdles I set for it. Is it time to buy the second half?

Shares of Amazon (AMZN +2.20%) trade around $250 as I write, more than 10% under their Aug. 3 closing high of $284.02. And they dipped after the company's third-quarter report on Oct. 29, even though Amazon had the highest operating margin in its history.

Before that report, I said I'd buy half a position ahead of it and the other half after. I also listed what would make me skip the second half: growth at Amazon Web Services (AWS) dropping back toward 20%, or operating income landing below management's guided range of $22.5 billion to $26.5 billion.

Neither happened. AWS grew 39%, and operating income was $28.1 billion. But the report also showed how fast the cost of that growth is climbing.

AWS, Amazon's cloud computing unit, is the company's profit engine. AWS revenue climbed 39% year over year to $46.0 billion for the third quarter. Growth has now picked up for six straight quarters, from 17% for the first quarter of 2025 to 18%, 20%, 24%, 28%, 37%, and now 39%. For a business with an annualized revenue run rate of $184 billion, I think this pace is remarkable.

AWS operating income climbed 55% to $17.8 billion, and the segment's operating margin widened to 38.7% from 34.6% for the third quarter of 2025. Sure, this margin did dip a little from the second quarter's 39.4%.

But the forward-looking number points the other way. AWS's backlog (customer commitments it hasn't yet booked as revenue) hit $578 billion, more than doubling year over year. And management said it thinks AWS can grow more than 40% in both 2027 and 2028.

Amazon's third-quarter operating income of $28.1 billion was up 61% from $17.4 billion a year ago, and it landed above even the top of the range management had guided to. Its operating margin of 13.9% compares to 9.7% for the year-ago quarter.

But the 61% number flatters the trend. Amazon's year-ago results included two special charges -- $2.5 billion for a legal settlement with the Federal Trade Commission and $1.8 billion in estimated severance costs. Without them, third-quarter 2025 operating income would have been $21.7 billion. By that measure, operating income grew around 29%.

It's still an impressive rate for a business with almost $800 billion in sales over the last 12 months. And the retail side pulled its weight. North America segment operating income climbed to $8.7 billion from $7.3 billion a year earlier excluding the settlement charge, or around 20% growth on sales that climbed 6%.

Amazon's cash capital expenditures (spending on data centers, servers, chips, and warehouses) hit $75.9 billion in the third quarter alone, over twice the year-ago number. Management still expects around $220 billion for 2026, up from about $128 billion in 2025.

"[E]xpect to spend meaningfully more in 2027 with a clear line of sight to attractive financial returns," CEO Andy Jassy said on the third-quarter earnings call.

This spending has turned Amazon's free cash flow into a big outflow. For the past 12 months, free cash flow was negative $42.4 billion, compared to negative $7.6 billion through the second quarter and positive $14.8 billion a year before. Operating cash flow, meanwhile, grew 29% to $168.3 billion over the past 12 months.

In other words, the company is taking in more cash than ever. Amazon is just spending more than all of it, mainly because of the artificial intelligence (AI) build-out. True, a $578 billion backlog arguably makes that spending look less like a gamble. But free cash flow could stay negative for a while if spending grows again in 2027, and I see that as the main risk with the stock.

The report didn't change my plan. Both things I said would decide the second half came through.

At around $250, Amazon's price-to-earnings ratio on analysts' 2027 estimates is about 23, down from about 24 when I spelled out the plan in late October. For a business whose underlying operating income rose about 29%, that valuation looks fair, even if the price still assumes the AI spending eventually turns into cash.

I'd go ahead and buy the second half of the position here. I'd just expect the free cash flow numbers to look rough for a while.
