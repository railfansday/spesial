Tanggal: 2026-11-03

The Chicago Bears and Seattle Seahawks will close out Week 8 of the NFL season on "Monday Night Football," with kickoff set for 8:15 p.m. ET at Lumen Field in Seattle.

The Seahawks find themselves at 4-3 to start the season with Sam Darnold at the helm, coming off a championship season. The team has dropped three of its past five games, but the Seahawks' offense will be getting reinforcements in Week 8. All-Pro guard Quenton Nelson will make his debut on the offensive line after last week's trade from the Indianapolis Colts.

Seattle will also get its top running backs in the mix as Zach Charbonnet makes his season debut after an ACL tear ended his 2025 campaign. First-round rookie Jadarian Price will also return to the lineup after a stint on injured reserve.

On the other sideline, All-Pro defensive tackle Jeffery Simmons will make his debut for the Bears after a blockbuster trade from the Tennessee Titans last week. The Bears paid a hefty price for Simmons, sending a package that included two first-round picks for the star. The Bears look to be going all in with franchise quarterback Caleb Williams on a rookie contract amid their 5-2 start.

The Seattle Seahawks haven't had a potent run game since the departure of Kenneth Walker III this offseason to the Kansas City Chiefs, but they'll get some reinforcements on Monday night that could make a difference moving forward.

Former second-round pick Zach Charbonnet will make his season debut on Monday night, who's returning from a torn ACL that ended his 2025 campaign in the playoffs. Charbonnet led the team with 12 rushing touchdowns in a complementary role to Walker last year and proved to be a reliable option along the goal line.

Price will make his return from injured reserve after suffering a chest injury in Week 3. He fumbled in back-to-back games before landing on IR, so ball security will presumably be an emphasis. He's averaged 4.3 yards per carry so far this season but is still seeking his first NFL touchdown.

The Bears sent the Titans 2027 and 2028 first-round picks on Thursday, along with a 2027 fourth-round pick, in exchange for the 29-year-old Simmons and a 2028 fifth-round pick. The transaction sent shockwaves through the NFL, igniting debates about whether the Bears overpaid or Simmons was worth the hefty price.

Poles clarified that the Simmons trade doesn't indicate a paradigm shift. More specifically, the Bears aren't adopting a front-office approach made famous by the Los Angeles Rams and their GM, Les Snead.

"I know it's kind of a joke that kind of goes around, but 'F them picks' is a big thing," Poles said. "That's really not what we're about. I know it took a lot to get this done, but we absolutely believe that the draft and bringing in young talent is a way that you sustain success around here."
