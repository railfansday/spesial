First Thing: US goes to polls in vote that will define the last years of Trump’s presidency

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Democrats are hoping to seize control in the Capitol and rein in the president’s power. Plus, a peacock that loves admiring itself in the mirror a little too much

Good morning. Americans are heading to the polls today in US midterm elections widely seen as a referendum on Donald Trump’s second presidency and a chance for opponents to rein in his power. The party holding the White House typically loses congressional seats at midterm elections and Democrats are hoping the president’s nose-diving popularity can be exploited.

Meanwhile, the US Department of Justice in California has charged six more people with alleged voter fraud crimes, an escalation of the administration’s campaign against election integrity. However, in a blow to Trump, the supreme court has ruled against attempts to force California election officials to segregate and not count ballots from people the justice department claims are non-citizens.

Lauren Gambino has been in Los Angeles, casting her eye over Xavier Becerra’s California shoo-in to be governer, while Joseph Gedeon offers this analysis from Detroit of how Israel is at the heart of Haley Stevens’s extraordinary reversal to back Abdul El-Sayed’s Republican opponent in Michigan.

The ⁠US has launched limited strikes on ​Iran’s Qeshm Island, hours after the vice-president, JD Vance, said he was optimistic that diplomatic efforts to end the war were moving in the right direction. The strikes targeted surface-to-air missiles being used against US assets, according to Reuters and the Associated Press. The US has targeted Qeshm Island on multiple occasions. Positioned in the mouth of the strait of Hormuz, the island has been used to launch attacks on shipping in the waterway.

European troops, including Nato members, would be allowed to fire back if attacked by Russians when potentially stationed as peacekeepers in Ukraine after a ceasefire, western officials have said. The news comes as Russia bombed an aid distribution centre and neighbouring shelter in southern Ukraine, killing at least 26 ​people.

US-led talks between Russia and Ukraine were due to resume next week, US special envoy Steve Witkoff said yesterday. The talks – to be held in Abu Dhabi – come as Kyiv and its European allies worry that Trump has increasingly sided with Moscow.

Savannah Guthrie has issued a new plea for help on the whereabouts of her 84-year-old mother, who authorities believe was abducted in February. Guthrie said: “We ache for her, we cry out for answers, we cannot move forward. We need someone to help us; tell us what you know. Our mom was a light in this world. We want to celebrate her and give her the dignity she deserves.”

An ex-FBI chief in Chicago is suing the Department of Justice, claiming he was put under pressure to investigate Trump’s foes. Douglas DePodesta says he was made a “scapegoat”.

Lindsay Clancy returned to court on Monday in an attempt to dismiss the charges against her that she murdered her three young children. Lawyers for the Massachusetts mother argued a new trial would violate double jeopardy rules.

A child in rural northern New York has died after contracting measles, the state’s first death from the disease in 30 years. In Pennsylvania, the governor, Josh Shapiro, is mounting a measles outreach program among the Amish community, which is “under-vaccinated”.

A Washington state resident has died after being hospitalized with “severe illness” after contracting bird flu, local health officials have said.

A 30-year-old man accused of starting the deadly Palisades fire is being retried for arson this week, four months after his first trial ended with a hung jury.

An ambulance driver in Ohio abandoned a patient they were taking to hospital after getting lost and decided to call an Uber and go home.

In its first earnings report for its new chief executive, John Ternus, Apple Inc posted stronger-than-expected revenue, bucking a downward trend for tech companies this quarter. Apple made $30.71bn in the July-September period, up 12% for last year’s figure.
