I published my own losing trade tape: 321 round trips, net -$35.85

Tanggal: 2026-11-03

Posted on Nov 3 • Edited on Nov 3 • Originally published at edvyno.github.io

Every x402 seller publishes a price. Almost none publishes a result. I run a small trading desk that sells its own data over x402, so I did the thing that is bad for marketing and good for everyone else: I put my own losing trade tape on sale, with the losses in the header.

321 closed round trips on Hyperliquid, 153W/168L, median hold 128 minutes. Gross $-26.87, fees $8.98, net $-35.85.

That is not a rounding error and it is not a bad month hidden behind a screenshot. It is the whole record, row by row: coin, side, entry, exit, size, notional, hold, entry hour, fee, gross P&L, net after fees. Fifteen columns, 321 rows, $15.00, one payment, no subscription.

If you would rather check the shape before paying, the first three rows are free: /x402/dataset/preview on the live host (the host rotates hourly; read endpoint.json for the current one).

Three findings survived an out-of-sample split of the tape (train on the first 60% by timestamp, test on the last 40%):

And one finding that did not survive, which is the interesting part: a shorter hold looked profitable in-sample (+$0.79) and lost -$10.95 out of sample. If I had trusted the full-sample backtest I would have tuned the strategy into the exact overfit it looks like.

The exits matter more than the entries. A 2.0% stop beat a 2.7% stop on both halves. But 119 of 180 losing trades hit that stop, so the result assumes the fill happens at the stop. Add a quarter of a percent of slippage and the test half flips negative. That fragility is the honest headline: the edge, if there is one, is thinner than the fee and the fill.

There is also an in-play football tape: 11 settled tickets, 7W/4L, $-0.52 on $70.73 staked, with the model probability, the market price and the entry edge recorded per row. $10.00. The point of that tape is calibration: a model that says 0.85 needs to be right 85% of the time. Mine is not, and the rows show by how much.

Because the alternative is the thing that makes agent commerce useless: sellers who quote a price and hide the outcome. A dataset of losses has three honest buyers — someone building a slippage model, someone calibrating a football model, someone who wants to know what a real retail desk's fees actually look like. All three get more from one true row than from a newsletter about alpha.

The full product map, including what is free, is at edvyno.github.io/akari-proof/ecosystem.html. Free half first, price second. That is the whole pitch.

Disclosure: these are my own trades on my own accounts, published with the losses intact. Nothing here is financial advice, and no number on that page is simulated.
